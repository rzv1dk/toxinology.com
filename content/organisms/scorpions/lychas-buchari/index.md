## Clinical Effects

Fatal outcome very unlikely

These small Buthid scorpions are the most frequent and serious cause of scorpion stings in Australia. Nearly all reliably reported cases involve Lychas marmoreus, although a few reorts of stings by L. variatus and some other Lychas species are available. The clinical presentation is the same throughout; the sting is felt, is painful, often severely so, pain lasting up to 2 or more hours. In the only reported large prospective study (Isbister et all; 2003) 90% of patients stung by Lychas spp. (mostly marmoreus) reported severe local pain. Other local effects include sting puncture marks (27%), swelling (14%), redness or a red mark (66%), tenderness (35%), numbness (12%) and parasthesiae (11%). 13% also reported systtemic effects, lasting around 12 hours (occasionally up to 24 hours). These systemic effects were all minor; headache, nausea, malaise. There is no evidence these scorpions are likely to cause major systemic effects such as cardiotoxicity, pulmonary oedema, or paralysis and they are not likely to prove lethal. The few early reports from Australia suggesting fatal outcomes are, most likely, in error.

### Coloration & Markings

Carapace, tergites and pedipalps are yellow to yellowish brown. Metasomal segments I to IV are brown on about the anterior one-quarter and yellowish brown posteriorly and segment V is black on about the anterior three-quarters and yellowish brown posteriorly. Telson is yellowish brown with a black spot surrounding the subaculear tooth. Ventral surface colour is pale yellowish. Legs are yellow to yellowish brown.

## First Aid

Keep victim still, apply pressure immobilisation bandage & splint, gently wash bite area, transport to medical care

First aid for scorpion stings (in areas where no potentially dangerous species may be found)

1. Reassure the victim. Despite sometimes intense local pain, usually lasting less than one hour, these scorpions are not able to cause major of lethal envenoming and most stings will settle rapidly.
1. Do not apply a bandage or tourniquet, or cut, suck or incise the wound or apply electric shock. Neither the application of local heat or cold are proven as effective.
1. If the victim develops general symptoms or the pain remains severe after one hour, seek medical advice.

## Medical Treatment

Stings by these scorpions are likely to cause only short lived local effects, such as pain, without systemic effects. Treatment is therefore reassurance and symptomatic, with analgesia only for severe and persistent pain, as short lived pain will likely resolve before analgesics can take effect. Update tetanus immune status. Be more cautious in young children, observing for several hours post-sting. No antivenom is available, or is required.

If the patient develops significant local or systemic effects, other than the expected local pain, re-evaluate and consider if the identity of the scorpion may be in error. Except for a few species of major medical importance, the effects of stings to humans by individual scorpion species is either unknown (most species) or poorly documented and therefore unexpected effects cannot be excluded for most "harmless" species.
