## Clinical Effects

Fatal outcome very unlikely

These small Australian Buthid scorpions are rarely reported as the cause of stings and do not cause major morbidity, let alone mortality. Even in the largest prospective study of Australian scorpion stings there are only 4 cases of confirmed Isometrus stings (Isbister et al; 2003). In only one case was a sting mark visible, only 2 of the 4 reported severe local pain and none reported any systemic symptoms. In another series of 3 cases (Flecker; 1937), all reported pain, described as severe, lasting up to 3 hours and becoming regional in one case, but none developed systemic symptoms.

### Coloration & Markings

Carapace, tergites, metasomal segments I to III and pedipalps are pale creamish brown to brown with variegated darker brown spotting. Metasomal segments IV and V are a distinctly darker brown. Telson vesicle and aculeus is reddish brown. Pedipalps are pale brown, Ventral surface and sternites are yellowish to pale brown.

## First Aid

First aid for scorpion stings (in areas where no potentially dangerous species may be found)

1. Reassure the victim. Despite sometimes intense local pain, usually lasting less than one hour, these scorpions are not able to cause major of lethal envenoming and most stings will settle rapidly.
1. Do not apply a bandage or tourniquet, or cut, suck or incise the wound or apply electric shock. Neither the application of local heat or cold are proven as effective.
1. If the victim develops general symptoms or the pain remains severe after one hour, seek medical advice.

## Medical Treatment

Stings cause predominantly local effects of short duration, usually not requiring medical treatment. No antivenom is available or indicated.
