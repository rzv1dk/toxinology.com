## Clinical Effects

A fatal outcome resulting from bites does not appear possible, and in most cases the injury will be mechanical, but may occasionally be severe. Secondary infection, which can occur after even minor bites, can also occasionally be severe.

This goanna is considered to be non-venomous, although its oral secretions contain products that have been found to resemble toxins, and bites are unlikely to cause medically significant envenoming (see also "Oral products of lizards - cautionary note" in the Overview Pages). There is a considerable amount of anecdotal experience with bites by these lizards and no indication significant systemic effects occur. Indeed, the major problems with bites is mechanical injury from multiple sharp teeth, that can be expected to cause significant pain. It is hard to determine if the oral products contribute to the pain. In addition, bites can develop secondary infection that can, on occasion, be quite severe.

## Description

### Adult Length

1.5 m

No data

### Coloration & Markings

Cream stripes running across the dorsal body in blotchy bands with very broad solid cream bands towards tail. Typically dirty although gives the appearance of being overall dark-brown or gray. Often bands of blue scales are found around the snout and in patches around the head. Ventrally yellow or cream with a few dark interspersed bands.

### Breeding

Oviparous, mating between November and January with eggs laid in termataria. Upon hatching females dig into termite mounds to release newly emerged hatchlings.

### Habits

Arboreal with a good ability for climbing especially when threatened however also spend considerable time on land. A diurnal heliotherm that refuges overnight in burrows before emerging in the early morning to begin a days activity. Active most of the year even in southern Australia however emergence time may vary with season.

Juveniles

Mass at hatching is about 25 g but may weigh as little as 16 g. Males mature when they reach a snout-vent-length of about 415 mm and females 385 mm.

Lethality

Not lethal although can inflict significant wounds.

### Prey

Intensive opportunistic forager that consumes a wide variety of different prey items including arthropods, macropods, rabbit, birds, reptiles and reptile eggs.

Sexual Dimorphism

No sexual dimorphism has been reported.

## First Aid

First aid for bites from lizards, other than helodermatid lizards (such as gila monster & beaded lizard).

1. Remove the patient from continued exposure to the lizard.
1. If the lizard is still attached to the patient (ie still biting) then carefully prize the jaws open. Do not pull the lizard off as this may increase the extent of skin injury as some of these lizards have very sharp teeth that may lacerate the skin.
1. Note the physical characteristics of the lizard so that, if necessary, it can be described to health care workers. If you have a camera, photograph the lizard.
1. Clean the wound with antiseptic, if available, otherwise use clean, preferably sterile water.
1. If the wound is deep, or there is extensive damage, or if significant pain persists after 30 minutes, seek medical care.
1. Tetanus infection is possible with any bite wound. If the patient does not have current tetanus immunisation, seek medical care.
1. Secondary infection is possible with any bite wound. If the wound initially appears minor, but over the next few days becomes red, tender to touch, more painful, swollen, or develops a discharge, secondary infection may have developed. In this case seek urgent medical care.
1. Systemic (general) effects from oral products of these animals are not expected. If the patient develops any systemic (general) symptoms, such as headache, vomiting, fever, dizziness, weakness, difficulty breathing, or any other general symptom, seek urgent medical care. If breathing is impaired, support using available methods, including mouth-to-mouth breathing if nothing else is available. It is emphasised that such systemic effects are not expected and not reported and this advice is given for completeness, not in the expectation it is likely to be required.

## Medical Treatment

Bites cause mechanical injury and sometimes secondary infection, with oral products playing, at most, a minor role, with no systemic effects. Therefore treatment is focussed on good wound care, including cleaning the wound, prevention of secondary infection, and ensuring tetanus immunisation is up to date.

## Toxin

Venom Components
B-type Natriuretic Protein (BNP): 2-4 KDa. Retention of residues necessary for natriuretic activity. Fry et al. (2006) (Ref : R001203).
Type III PLA2: 15 KDa. Inhibition of platelet aggregation. Fry et al. (2006) (Ref : R001203).
CRISP: 23-25 KDa. Loop I doublet (KR) found which is believed to be an essential part of the blockage of cyclic-nucleotide-gated calcium channels. Most isoforms also found to have a loop I motif (EXXF) thought to contribute to the inhibition of smooth muscle contraction through the blockage of L-type Ca2+ channels. Fry et al. (2006) (Ref : R001203).
Kallikrein: 23-25 KDa. Showed a high conservation of functional residues and cysteine spacing. Fry et al. (2006) (Ref : R001203).
Crude Venom

cDNA sequencing and LC/MS analysis indicated high concentrations of kallikrein and BNP-type natriuretic toxins in V. varius oral secretions. Intravenous injections of crude oral products into anaesthetized rats rapidly produced a sharp drop in blood pressure. Anaylsis with precontracted rat aortic rings demonstrated BNP relaxs aortic smooth muscle Fry et al. (2006) (Ref : R001203).

Other Toxins

B-type Natriuretic Protein (BNP): Retention of residues necessary for natriuretic activity or the excretion of an excessively large amount of sodium in the urine.

Neurotoxins Channel Toxins

CRISP: 23-25 KDa. Loop I doublet (KR) found which is believed to be an essential part of the blockage of cyclic-nucleotide-gated calcium channels. Most isoforms also found to have a loop I motif (EXXF) thought to contribute to the inhibition of smooth muscle contraction through the blockage of L-type Ca2+ channels.

Venom Activity

Varanid type III PLA2 (Phospholipase) toxin has been shown to inhibit platelet aggregation much like Heloderma type III PLA2 isolated from Heloderma suspectum and H. horridum. Consistent with Heloderma type III PLA2 included the preservation of cysteines and cysteine spacing as well as the conservation of functional residues. Fry et al. (2006) (Ref :R001203).

Haematological Haemorrhagins

Type III PLA2: Inhibition of platelet aggregation. Fry et al. (2006) (Ref : R001203).

Kallikrein: Showed a high conservation of functional residues and cysteine spacing. Fry et al. (2006) (Ref : R001203).
