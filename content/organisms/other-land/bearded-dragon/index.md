## Clinical Effects

A fatal outcome resulting from bites does not appear possible, and in most cases the injury will be mechanical, but may occasionally be severe. Secondary infection, which can occur after even minor bites, can also occasionally be severe.

This dragon lizard is considered to be non-venomous, although its oral secretions contain products that have been found to resemble toxins, and bites are unlikely to cause medically significant envenoming (see also "Oral products of lizards - cautionary note" in the Overview Pages). There is a considerable amount of anecdotal experience with bites by these lizards and no indication significant systemic effects occur. Indeed, the major problems with bites is mechanical injury from multiple sharp teeth, that can be expected to cause significant pain. It is hard to determine if the oral products contribute to the pain. In addition, bites can develop secondary infection that can, on occasion, be quite severe.

## Description

### Adult Length

### Coloration & Markings

Colour variable from pale gray to fawn or brown, reddish-brown or almost black above. Series of pale oblong blotches in two longitudinal series, one either side of the mid-dorsal line. Usually a dark postocular stripe with whitish throat and tail often banded.

### Breeding

Oviparous.

### Habits

Often seen during the day perched on fallen timber, stumps, fence posts or roadside verges. Regularly encountered basking on roads. Mature males have been found to display a level of territoriality.

Lethality

Not lethal but can inflict a painful bite.

### Prey

Insects, flowers and soft herbs.

## First Aid

First aid for bites from lizards, other than helodermatid lizards (such as gila monster & beaded lizard).

1. Remove the patient from continued exposure to the lizard.
1. If the lizard is still attached to the patient (ie still biting) then carefully prize the jaws open. Do not pull the lizard off as this may increase the extent of skin injury as some of these lizards have very sharp teeth that may lacerate the skin.
1. Note the physical characteristics of the lizard so that, if necessary, it can be described to health care workers. If you have a camera, photograph the lizard.
1. Clean the wound with antiseptic, if available, otherwise use clean, preferably sterile water.
1. If the wound is deep, or there is extensive damage, or if significant pain persists after 30 minutes, seek medical care.
1. Tetanus infection is possible with any bite wound. If the patient does not have current tetanus immunisation, seek medical care.
1. Secondary infection is possible with any bite wound. If the wound initially appears minor, but over the next few days becomes red, tender to touch, more painful, swollen, or develops a discharge, secondary infection may have developed. In this case seek urgent medical care.
1. Systemic (general) effects from oral products of these animals are not expected. If the patient develops any systemic (general) symptoms, such as headache, vomiting, fever, dizziness, weakness, difficulty breathing, or any other general symptom, seek urgent medical care. If breathing is impaired, support using available methods, including mouth-to-mouth breathing if nothing else is available. It is emphasised that such systemic effects are not expected and not reported and this advice is given for completeness, not in the expectation it is likely to be required.

## Medical Treatment

Bites cause mechanical injury and sometimes secondary infection, with oral products playing, at most, a minor role, with no systemic effects. Therefore treatment is focussed on good wound care, including cleaning the wound, prevention of secondary infection, and ensuring tetanus immunisation is up to date.

## Toxin

Venom Components
Sequenced from the mandibular and maxillary gland of Pogona barbata. Fry et al. (2006) (Ref : R001203). Prior to this findings Crotamine had only previously been identified from rattlesnake venoms (Crotalus).
Myotoxins

Crotamine, Fry et al. (2006) (Ref : R001203): 42 AA toxin cross-linked protein with three disulfide bridges which has been shown to modify voltage-sensitive Na+ channels and exhibits analgesic activity and can be myonecrotic.

Other Studies

Pogona barbata found to have both incipient mandibular and maxillary glands characteristic of an ancestral venom system in reptiles. These glands were found to contain Crotamine. Fry et al (2006) (Ref : R001203).
