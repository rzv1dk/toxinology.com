## First Aid

First aid for Starfish stings ( Sea Star )

1. The victim should immediately leave the water.
1. Any broken spines should be removed and any sting spines (pedicellariae) attached to the skin should be removed using forceps, if available.
1. The wound should not be rubbed, or spines crushed as this may worsen the local effects of the sting. Do not apply a pressure bandage, tourniquet or cut or suck the wound.
1. The use of immersion in hot water is controversial. Some authorities suggest that immersing the stung limb in hot water may reduce pain. Others suggest it is useless. It is potentially hazardous, if used incorrectly, as it may cause burn injuries. If used, immerse the opposite limb to that stung in water which is hot, but bearable with prolonged contact. It should not be so hot as to cause pain. Then immerse the stung limb as far as necessary to include the sting area. Keep both limbs in the water for 15-20 minutes, providing there is relief of pain. If there is no relief of pain in this time, abandon the immersion. If pain has subsided, it may return on removal from the water, in which case, reimmerse for a further 15-20 minutes. Repeat this process up to 4 times. If after 1-2 hours, the pain is still severe on removal from hot water, then other forms of pain relief may be required, but these will require medical intervention.
1. The wound may be gently cleaned with soapy water.
1. Elevation of the stung limb, after completion of immersion treatment, may reduce any developing swelling.

### Haematological Haemorrhagins

AP-PLA2-I : AP-PLA2-I is a dimer composed of the same subunit with Mol. Wt. = 12,000 ( gel filtration ) to 15,000 ( SDS-PAGE ). The N-terminal 62 AA residues were sequenced and indicated AP-PLA2-I has a high Asx, Glx and Gly content. AP-PLA2-I was identified as a class 1 enzyme ( presence of Cys-11, absence of Cys-51 and presence of the elapid loop in the region of 53-61 ). Additional minor amino acids found in 17 positions suggested the co-existence of another PLA2 component. AP-PLA2-I showed haemolytic activity only in the presence of phosphatidicholine and released fluorescent fatty acids from phosphatidicholine with labeled fatty acids at the sn-2 position. Enzymatic activity was enhanced by Ca++ and reduced by Cu++ and Zn++. Shiomi et al. (1998) ( Ref : R001155 ).

AP-PLA2-II : AP-PLA2-II is a monomer with Mol. Wt. = 12,000 ( gel filtration ) to 15,000 ( SDS-PAGE ). The N-terminal 62 AA residues were sequenced and indicated AP-PLA2-II has a high Asx, Glx and Gly content. The N-terminal sequence shares 65% identity with AP-PLA2-I. AP-PLA2-II was identified as a class 1 enzyme ( presence of Cys-11, absence of Cys-51 and presence of the elapid loop in the region of 53-61 ). AP-PLA2-II showed haemolytic activity only in the presence of phosphatidicholine and released fluorescent fatty acids from phosphatidicholine with labeled fatty acids at the sn-2 position. Enzymatic activity was enhanced by Ca++ and reduced by Cu++ and Zn++. Shiomi et al. (1998) ( Ref : R001155 ).

Plancinin. About 1.4% of crude venom, Mol. Wt. =7,500 in native form and Mol. Wt. = 3,000 under reduced conditions in the presence of mercaptoethanol. Plancinin is a dimeric peptide with a disulfide bridge. It is an anticoagulant ( a 6.5 fold increase in anticoagulant activity of crude venom ) with increased fibrin formation time, without effect on human platelet aggregation or vascular permeability. Injection of plancinin ( 1.5 mg / kg ) in mice resulted in prolonged bleeding times of 1 to 2 hours and recovery to normal levels in 3 hours. Karasudani et al. (1996) ( Ref : R001156 ).

Lethal Factor. Mol. Wt. = 20,000 ( gel filtration ) to 25,000 ( SDS-disc electrophoresis ) and isoelectric point pI = 10.6 The lethal factor is a basic glycoprotein ( but a rich acidic amino acid content ) with a 3.5 % carbohydrate content. It also has weak haemolytic ( 18.2 Hu / mg against sheep erythrocytes ), oedema forming ( 65 µg of lethal factor caused edema of 123% i.m injection in mouse foot pad and 130 µg intradermal injection in rat skin caused a blue weal 5 mm in diameter ) and capillary permeability-increasing activities but no haemorrhagic or PLA2 activities were detected. Shiomi et al. (1988) ( Ref : R001157 ). Change in liver colour, swelling of the gall bladder and jaundice were observed at autopsy following injection of mice with lethal factor. Several serum and hepatic enzymes were markedly ( and abnormally ) elevated or reduced. Histopathological examination revealed severe degenerative changes including enlargement and necrosis of hepatocytes in liver tissue suggesting that the lethal factor is a potent hepatotoxin. Shiomi et al. (1990) ( Ref : R001158 ).
