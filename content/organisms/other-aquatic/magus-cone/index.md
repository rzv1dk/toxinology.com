## Clinical Effects

Fatal outcome potentially possible

There is no clinical information on stings by most cone shell species and it is likely that many are not capable of injuring humans. The following is based on experience with those few species known to have caused envenoming in humans. It should not be assumed that this species' will show such severe effects, but should envenoming occur, it is most likely to fit the pattern described below.

Cone snail stings are potentially painful, but in many cases the sting causes only minor pain and may even not be noticed. When pain is present it is occasionally moderately severe. Often numbness develops around the sting area. There may be bleeding from around the sting site, Local swelling is possible, but not common.

The most important symptoms are systemic. A variable time after the sting, but potentially quite rapidly, in less than 60 mins, progressive flaccid paralysis develops, with ptosis, dysarthria, dysphagia, limb weakness and respiratory paralysis, resulting in hypoxia, coma or death in severe cases. The progression from first signs to full respiratory paralysis varies from less than 60 mins to many hours. Not all cases will progress from minor flaccid paralysis to full paralysis. Reversal of paralysis may take several hours, occasionally several days.

### Special Risk Groups
#### Children

Risk to children from local or systemic envenoming is similar in nature to the risk for adults, but a childs smaller body mass implies that the comparitive venom concentration will be higher, so more severe envenoming is likely, the increased risk being inversely proportional to the size of the child.

#### Pregnancy

Potential risk to foetus from systemic envenoming, similar to risk to mother

#### Elderly

The higher incidence of pre-existing diseases in the elderly and associated frailty place them at higher risk should envenoming occur. The nature and extent of increased risk will largely be determined by the nature and extent of pre-existing disease and by any medications used to treat such disease.

## First Aid

Keep victim still, apply pressure immobilisation bandage & splint, gently wash bite area, transport to medical care

First aid for Conus (cone snail) stings

1. After ensuring the patient and onlookers have moved out of range of further strikes by the cone snail, the bitten person should be reassured and persuaded to lie down and remain still. Some will be terrified, fearing sudden death and, in this mood, they may behave irrationally or even hysterically. The basis for reassurance is the fact that many cone snail stings do not result in envenoming and death is a rare outcome.
1. The sting wound should not be tampered with in any way. Wiping it once with a damp cloth to remove surface venom is unlikely to do much harm (or good) but the wound must not be massaged.
1. All rings or other jewellery on the bitten limb, especially on fingers, should be removed, as they may act as tourniquets if oedema develops.
1. If the bite is on a limb, a broad bandage (even torn strips of clothing or pantyhose) should be applied over the bitten area at moderate pressure (as for a sprain; not so tight circulation is impaired), then extended to cover as much of the bitten limb as possible, including fingers or toes, going over the top of clothing rather than risking excessive limb movement by removing clothing. The bitten limb should then be immobilised as effectively as possible using an extemporised splint or sling.
1. If there is any impairment of vital functions, such as problems with respiration, airway, circulation, heart function, these must be supported as a priority. In particular, for bites causing flaccid paralysis, including respiratory paralysis, both airway and respiration may be impaired, requiring urgent and prolonged treatment, which may include the mouth to mask (mouth to mouth) technique of expired air transfer. Seek urgent medical attention.
1. Do not use Tourniquets, cut, suck or scarify the wound or apply chemicals or electric shock.
1. Avoid peroral intake, absolutely no alcohol. No sedatives outside hospital. If there will be considerable delay before reaching medical aid, measured in several hours to days, then give clear fluids by mouth to prevent dehydration.
1. If the offending cone snail has been killed it should be brought with the patient for identification, but be careful to avoid touching a still living cone snail, as it can sting anyone attempting to pick it up. There are no safe places to hold a cone snail. If in doubt, either leave it alone or use an object such as a stick to push it into a sealable container.
1. The cone snail sting victim should be transported as quickly and as passively as possible to the nearest place where they can be seen by a medically-trained person (health station, dispensary, clinic or hospital). The bitten limb must not be exercised as muscular contraction will promote systemic absorption of venom. If no motor vehicle or boat is available, the patient can be carried on a stretcher or hurdle, on the pillion or crossbar of a bicycle or on someone's back.
1. Most traditional, and many of the more recently fashionable, first aid measures are useless and potentially dangerous. These include local cauterization, incision, excision, amputation, suction by mouth, vacuum pump or syringe, combined incision and suction ("venom-ex" apparatus), injection or instillation of compounds such as potassium permanganate, phenol (carbolic soap) and trypsin, application of electric shocks or ice (cryotherapy), use of traditional herbal, folk and other remedies including the ingestion of emetic plant products and parts of the snake, multiple incisions, tattooing and so on.

## Medical Treatment

Treatment for significant cone snail envenoming is support of respiratory function and management of hypotension, associated with rapid, potentially lethal flaccid neurotoxic paralysis. No antivenom is available.

While most cone snail stings, even by the few species known to have envenomed humans, will be minor, lack of case data accross the wide range of species and the fact that envenoming can be rapidly lethal, mandate that all cases be urgently assessed and admitted for at least 12-24 hrs, until it is clear no flaccid paralysis has occurred.

No antivenom is available, so treatment is focussed on supporting vital functions. The main risks are potentially rapid and severe flaccid paralysis, including full respiratory paralysis, and possibly secondary hypotension and cardiotoxicity, plus sequelae of major shock, should that occur. The principle risk is respiratoey failure due to paralysis. Intubation and mechanical ventilation may be required, but it appears paralysis is measured in hours, rarely a day or so, not the many days to weeks seen with some paralytic snake venoms.

### Venom Components

- ~a-Conotoxin MI [ Syn : ~a-MI , ~a-Ctx-MI ]. ( postsynaptic ). 14 AA with four cysteine residues and mass 1493 Da. Gray et al. (1983) (Ref: R007742) McIntosh et al. (1982) ( Ref : R000954 ).
- ~a-Conotoxin MII [ Syn : ~a-MII , ~a-Ctx-MII ]. 16 AA polypeptide with mass 1710.6 Da and two disulfide bridges which form a hydrophobic cysteine knot. It targets ~a3~b2 mammalian nicotine ACh receptors. Cartier et al. (1996) ( Ref : R001178 ) Hill et al. (1998) (Ref: R007709) Shon et al. (1997) (Ref: R007710).
- ~w-Conotoxin MVIIA ( presynaptic ). 25 AA peptide with 3 disulfide bridges, N-type Ca++ channel blocker. Discriminates between Ca2+ channel subtypes. Basus et al. (1995) (Ref: R007711. Wen et al. (2005) (Ref: R007694) Atkinson et al. (2000) (Ref: R007708) Olivera et al. (1987) (Ref: R007638) Norton and Olivera (2006) (Ref: R008272). Analgesic and neuroprotective properties as a result of inhibition of the release of neurotransmitters and suppression of secondary consequences of massive calcium influx after ischemia and mechanical trauma of the brain. Rajendra et al. (2004)(Ref: R007748). Decreases arteriol blood pressure in conscious rats in a dose-dependent manner when injected i.v. Bowersox et al. (1992) (Ref: R007624).
- ~w-Conotoxin MVIIC. 26 AA peptide with 3 disulfide bridges, selective mammalian Q-type Ca++ channel blocker. Wen et al. (2005) (Ref: R007694) Rajendra et a. (2004) (Ref: R007748).
- ~w-Conotoxin TX
- ~d-Conotoxin MVIA. O-superfamily Conotoxin 32 AA with six cysteine residues. Bulaj et al. (2001) (Ref: R007684).
- ~d-Conotoxin MVIC. O-superfamily Conotoxin 32 AA with six cysteine residues. Bulaj et al. (2001) (Ref: R007684).
- ~d-Conotoxin MVIB. O-superfamily Conotoxin 32 AA with six cysteine residues. Bulaj et al. (2001) (Ref: R007684).
- ~d-Conotoxin MVID. O-superfamily Conotoxin 32 AA with six cysteine residues. Bulaj et al. (2001) (Ref: R007684).
- ~m-Conotoxin MIIIA. 26 AA with 6 cysteine residues which targets tetrodotoxin-resistant sodium channels. Zhang et al. (2006) (Ref: R007707).
- ~w-conotoxin MVIID. 25 AA with six cysteine residues which targets other voltage-gated calcium channels besides the N-subtype. Monje et al. (1993) (Ref: R007615).
- Myotoxin. [Syn MTX]. Elicits complete loss of contractile response of the mouse diaphragm to electrical stimulation, an increase in the contractile force of the guinea-pig left atria, a tonic contraction of the guinea-pig taenia caeci and powerful rhythmic contractions of the guinea-pig ileum and vas deferens. These excitatory effected are blocked by tetrodotoxin. Kobayashi et al. (1983) (Ref: R007639).
### Crude Venom

Crude Venom : Venom extracts were shown to be toxic to mice ( minimum lethal dose was 0.2 to 0.7 mg / kg ), toxic to blennies ( minimum lethal dose was about 0.3 mg / kg ), non-toxic to Phyllodoce - worms ( 50 ~mg venom per dose ) and lethal to Melarapha - gastropods ( 50 ~mg venom per dose ) but without causing paralysis. Endean and Rudkin (1965) ( Ref : R000979 ).

Effects of crude venom ( 0.001 g / ml and 20 minutes exposure time ) were studied on mouse isolated diaphragm, guinea pig isolated left atria, guinea pig ileum and rabbit isolated aorta. An excitatory effect causing potent rhythmic, transient contractions of guinea pig ileum followed by a potent inhibitory effect and relaxation was elicited ( > 50% excitation and > 50% inhibition of control response ). A potent excitatory effect followed by a potent inhibitory effect was elicited on guinea pig isolated left atria ( > 50% excitation and > 50% inhibition of control response ). A potent inhibitory effect was elicited on mouse diaphragm ( > 50% inhibition of control response ). A moderate excitatory effect was elicited on rabbit aorta ( 20 to 50% excitation of control response ). Kobayashi et al. (1982) ( Ref : R000949 ).

Crude venom also mildly inhibits the binding of NPY to calf hippocampal and frontal cortex membranes Czerwiec et al. (1996) (Ref: R007845).

### Neurotoxins Channel Toxins

~a-Conotoxin M1 : M1 is a 14 AA residue peptide toxin, with 2 disulfide bonds and sequence { Gly Arg Cys Cys His Pro Ala Cys Gly Lys Asn Tyr Ser Cys NH2 } isolated by McIntosh et al. (1982) ( Ref : R000954 ). Binding experiments ( Torpedo electric organ as test tissue ) using 125I-~a-bungarotoxin as a probe showed that ~a-Conotoxins SI ( C. striatus, M1 and G1 ( from C. geographus ) were capable of displacing all specific binding to the Torpedo receptor with similar affinity ( IC50 = 0.42, 0.30 and 0.55 respectively. Zafaralla et al. (1988) ( Ref : R001172 ). ~a-Conotoxin M1 was tested for neuromuscular transmission and effects on the cardiovascular system in anaesthetised cats and compared with ~a-Conotoxin G1. Neuromuscular blockade was produced at 20 to 80 ~mg / kg ( twitch responses of the tibialis anterior and soleus muscles in response to stimulation of the sciatic nerve ) and was rapidly reversed by the anticholinesterase neostigmine. At doses that abolished neuromuscular transmission, ~a-Conotoxin M1 had no effect on arterial blood pressure, heart rate or responses to vagal and preganglionic stimulation, indicating specificity for nicotinic receptors at the neuromuscular junction. ~a-Conotoxin M1 has about 40% the potency of ~a-Conotoxin G1. Marshall and Harvey (1990) ( Ref : R000952 ).

~a-Conotoxin MII : ~a-Conotoxin MII is 16 AA residue peptide with 2 disulfide bridges and Mol. Wt. = 1,711. ~a-Conotoxin MII blocks the response to ACh in Xenopus oocytes expressing the ~a3~b2 nicotinic ACh receptors with an IC50 = 0.5 nM. Intraperitoneal injection of 10 nmol of ~a-Conotoxin MII into 8-10 g mice did not cause any sign of paralysis. Intramuscular injection of 5 nmol of ~a-Conotoxin MII into fish id not cause any sign of paralysis. Cartier et al. (1996) ( Ref : R001178 ).

~d-Conotoxin MVIA. O-superfamily Conotoxin 32 AA with six cysteine residues. Inhibits inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels. Bulaj et al. (2001) (Ref: R007684).

~d-Conotoxin MVIC. O-superfamily Conotoxin 32 AA with six cysteine residues. Inhibits inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels. Bulaj et al. (2001) (Ref: R007684).

~d-Conotoxin MVIB. O-superfamily Conotoxin 32 AA with six cysteine residues. Inhibits inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels. Bulaj et al. (2001) (Ref: R007684).

~d-Conotoxin MVID. O-superfamily Conotoxin 32 AA with six cysteine residues. Inhibits inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels. Bulaj et al. (2001) (Ref: R007684).

~m-Conotoxin MIIIA. Targets tetrodotoxin-resistant sodium channels (TTX-r and TTX-s) in a slowly reversible manner. Zhang et al. (2006) (Ref: R007707).

~w-Conotoxin MVIIA ( presynaptic ). N-type Ca++ channel blocker. Inhibits depolarization-activated Ba 2+ currents recorded from oocytes expressing N-type but not L- or R-type calcium channels. Adams et al. (2003) (Ref: R007759) Wen et al. (2005) (Ref: R007694) Atkinson et al. (2000) (Ref: R007708). Inhibits K+-evoked release of the pro-nociceptive neurotransmitter, SP, from rat spinal cord slices in a concentration-dependant manner. Smith et al. (2002) (Ref: R007760).

~w-Conotoxin TX. Inhibits 80 % of the depolarization-evoked ATP release with half maximal inhibition of ATP release occurring with 0.5 ~mM. Effects are reversible and when toxin is removed dependence of recovery of release is approximately first order and half complete within 40 minutes Yeager et al. (1987) (Ref: R007743).
