- Abnormal Haemostasis and Bleeding
- Effects on Red Blood Cells (potentially including haemolysis)
- Effects on White Blood Cells (notably Leukocytosis and/or Lymphopenia)
- Effects on Platelets (increase, decrease, change in platelet aggregation)
- Myotoxic (local or systemic muscle damage)
- Paralytic Neurotoxicity
- Excitatory Neurotoxicity
- Dermatological Effects
- Primary Necrotoxicity at bite/sting/contact location
- Cardiovascular Effects
- Respiratory Effects
- Angio Oedema or Major Allergic Reaction

## First Aid

First aid for marine coelenterate injuries (Jellyfish)

1. Remove the victim from the water, at all times being aware of the potential for further stings, both to the victim and rescuers. In areas where box jellyfish may occur, if possible wear protective clothing, such as stinger suits or pantyhose. Be careful not to touch undischarged tentacles on the victim with bare skin, as this may result in the rescuer also being stung.
1. Unless the sting is known or highly likely to be from a box jellyfish or close relative, do not douse the area in vinegar, as this may discharge unfired stinging cells.
1. Apply a cold pack to the stung area. Do not use cold or fresh water, as this may cause stinging cell discharge.
1. Assess vital functions, especially airway, breathing and circulation. Apply respiratory and cardiac support, as appropriate. If there is cardiac arrest, commence full resuscitation in an optimistic fashion and continue until medical care is reached.
1. If available, give oxygen. Entonox (nitrous oxide), if available, may be used for pain.
1. If the victim is in severe pain or shows signs of a significant general reaction, urgently transport the victim to medical care. If the reaction is mild and the pain settles readily, it may not be necessary to seek medical advice, but if in any doubt, seek advice.
